Wander Message Streams

From Independent Channels to Orchestrated Communication

Author Growth Engineering Date March 5, 2026 Audience Kyle, Brian, Sorrell Proposal
A Wander user doesn't experience "the Customer.io team" or "the Outreach team." They experience Wander. When they get a welcome email at 9am, a BDR cold email at 11am, and a newsletter at 2pm — all on the same day — they experience a company that doesn't know what it's doing.

Today we have good tools, good people, and good intentions. What we're missing is the connective tissue between them: shared rules for who talks to a user, when, and what happens when two teams want to talk to the same person at the same time.

This document proposes a shared vocabulary for that problem, describes where we are today, and outlines what coordinated messaging could look like.

Overarching Concept

Wander messaging should be treated as one Communication Orchestrator, not separate campaigns by team.

1) Two-tier channel model

  • Tier 1 (decisioning channels): Onboarding, Newsletter/Topics, Property Recommendations, Sales Outreach, Transactional, Concierge.
  • Tier 2 (downstream amplification channels): Ads, Social, and later Video.

Tier 2 should not decide independently. It should consume eligibility and suppression state from Tier 1.

2) Ads are part of orchestration

  • Ads must follow the same suppression and frequency logic as email and sales outreach.
  • Example: if a user is in onboarding or an active booking window, prospecting ads should be paused or narrowed.

3) Video is downstream

  • Video is a content format/distribution channel, not a separate decision engine.
  • We decide who should be messaged upstream; video decides how that message is delivered downstream.

4) Newsletter topic subscription source of truth

  • Customer.io should be the writable source of truth for topic subscriptions and channel preferences.
  • The wander.com UI in the Next.js app should be a wrapper interface: User updates preferences in app → backend writes to Customer.io profile attributes/lists → backend emits events to Segment for audit/history.
  • Avoid dual writable truth between app DB and Customer.io.

5) Practical implication

This is not just "better campaigns." It is shared state + explicit rules across channels:

  • Enrollment rules
  • Suppression and precedence rules
  • Handoff rules
  • Frequency caps
  • Observability of what each user has received across all channels

Key Concepts

Enrollment Rules
What puts a user into a stream. Today these are implicit — someone set up a campaign trigger months ago and it just runs. We need them explicit.
Suppression Rules
The most critical missing piece. "When stream A is active, pause stream B." Two flavors: HARD must not send, SOFT should avoid sending within a time window.
Conflict Zones
A phase where multiple streams overlap and no suppression rule exists. These are where users get a bad experience. We've identified five.
Handoff Rules
What happens when a user transitions between phases — which streams start, which stop, and what data gets passed.

Part 2: Where We Are Today

Current concerns

  • No centralized naming, planning, or stream inventory.
  • Fear of "double touch" from overlapping streams.
  • Lack of design and brand cohesion between emails.
  • Customer.io to Outreach sync (and reverse sync) is incomplete.
  • No shared user-state model (onboarding, sales-engaged, booked, in-trip, etc.).
  • No explicit stream precedence when streams conflict.
  • Inconsistent trigger and exit definitions across teams.
  • No global frequency caps across all channels.
  • Weak observability into "what this user received this week."
  • Limited auditability for suppressions and handoffs.
  • Manual handoffs between Marketing, Sales, and Concierge are brittle.
  • Sync latency/failure risk can create stale or conflicting state.
  • Deliverability risk from overlapping campaigns near booking windows.
  • Unclear ownership and escalation path for orchestration incidents.

The good

  • Customer.io is well-configured with 172 tracked events, solid campaign infrastructure, and a working abandoned cart flow.
  • Outreach has active BDR sequences with CIO webhook integration for lead handoff.
  • Transactional emails fire reliably on booking events from the backend.
  • The growth platform handles CIO ↔ Outreach sync, BDR assignment, and property recs.

The gaps

Here's what's actually happening to users today:

1 No suppression during onboarding

When a user signs up, the onboarding sequence starts in CIO. But there's nothing stopping a newsletter from also going out that week, or property recs from firing if they view 3+ properties during their first session.

Result: New user gets welcome email (day 0), newsletter (day 2), and "properties like the ones you viewed" (day 3). Three emails in three days from three different "senders." The onboarding narrative is broken.
Missing: A CIO segment or attribute that marks users as "in onboarding" and suppresses other campaigns.
2 Sales and marketing don't know about each other

When a user qualifies for BDR outreach, Sorrell's team picks them up in Outreach. But CIO doesn't know this happened. The user continues receiving automated marketing emails while a BDR is building a personal 1:1 relationship.

Result: BDR sends a personalized "I noticed you were looking at Tahoe" email. Two hours later, CIO sends "Top 10 Beach Houses This Spring!" The personal touch is undercut.
Missing: Outreach enrollment status synced back to CIO, so marketing campaigns can suppress.
3 Booking creates a message flood

When Order Completed fires, multiple streams activate simultaneously: transactional (confirmation, receipt), concierge (welcome), newsletter (regular schedule), and property recs (still running from browsing).

Result: User books a trip and gets 3-4 emails within 48 hours from different parts of Wander.
Missing: Post-booking suppression window for marketing. Coordination between concierge and transactional.
4 Concierge and system step on each other

The concierge team manually handles check-in coordination. The transactional system also sends automated check-in instructions. Neither knows what the other has sent.

Result: Guest gets a warm personal check-in message at 2pm, then an automated "Your check-in instructions" at 4pm with the same information.
Missing: A flag marking when concierge has handled a touchpoint, so the automated message can be skipped.
5 Post-trip is a free-for-all

After checkout, everything fires at once: review request, concierge follow-up, newsletter resumes, property recs resume. No sequencing.

Result: Four emails in three days, right when inbox goodwill from the trip is at its peak.
Missing: A post-trip sequencing plan that spaces messages and prioritizes the highest-value touchpoint first.

The root cause

These aren't tool problems. CIO can suppress campaigns. Outreach can sync data. The backend can set flags.

Each stream was built independently, by different people, at different times, with no shared orchestration layer. There's no place where the rules live, no place where "is this user in a sales sequence?" is checked before sending a marketing email.

Part 3: Proposed Future

The goal: Any Wander user, at any point in their lifecycle, receives messages that feel like they're coming from one coordinated team — not six independent systems racing to their inbox.

Principle: Fewer, better messages

The instinct is always to add more messages. The higher-leverage move is to remove messages that conflict and sequence messages that complement each other. A user who gets 2 perfectly-timed emails trusts Wander more than a user who gets 6 overlapping ones.

Content Wheel Baseline (Marketplace)

To operationalize this for wander.com, run a fixed baseline and layer "special" interventions on top.

Newsletter (general)1 per week
Newsletter (Wander recs)1 per week

Source content for derivatives: blog posts, videos, customer stories, operator stories, and specials (human intervention).

"Special" slot policy: every special must use one explicit slot rule.

  • Replace: push planned content by 1 slot.
  • Insert: add an extra bulletin without moving baseline slots.

At ~25 blog posts/month, the risk is not too little content. The risk is uncoordinated distribution. The orchestrator must control audience eligibility, suppression, and frequency caps across email + ads + social.

What Needs to Change

Customer.io (Brian)

  1. Add lifecycle attributes: onboarding_active, outreach_sequence_active, has_active_booking, concierge_handled_checkin, last_transactional_email_at
  2. Add suppression checks to newsletter and recs campaigns using those attributes
  3. Post-booking suppression window: 48h suppress on Order Completed

Outreach (Sorrell)

  1. Webhook on sequence enrollment/completion → growth platform → CIO attribute sync
  2. Visibility into CIO state before manual outreach (booking status, onboarding, concierge)

Growth Platform (Engineering)

  1. Attribute sync service: Outreach webhooks → CIO attribute updates
  2. Booking lifecycle hooks: Order Completed / checkout → CIO attributes
  3. Concierge flag API: Slack command or endpoint for "I've handled check-in for booking X"

Transactional System (Engineering)

  1. Write last_transactional_email_at to CIO on every send
  2. Check concierge_handled_checkin before automated check-in email
